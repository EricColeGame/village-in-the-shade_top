## What Is Village in the Shade?

Village in the Shade is a single-player farming simulation from Nippon Ichi Software, the studio behind the Yomawari horror series, published in the West by [NIS America](https://www.nisamerica.com/). Known in Japan as *Hono Gurashi no Niwa*, it drops you into Kagatsu, a secluded mountain village built on farming, seasonal festivals, and traditions that nobody quite explains at first.

By day you grow crops, raise animals, fish, hunt, forage, cook, craft furniture, and get to know the locals. By night, the village has opinions about where you should be — and those opinions are the whole hook.

If you have played a farm sim before, the daytime rhythm will feel familiar. What separates this one is that the cozy half and the unsettling half share the same map, the same clock, and the same save file.

## Pick Your Mode Before You Plant Anything

The single most important early decision has nothing to do with crops. At the start of the game you choose between the standard experience and **Village in the Sunshine**, a safer option that earlier materials referred to as Peaceful Life Mode.

In Village in the Sunshine, you can farm and socialize without being able to break the village code. In the standard experience, breaking the rules is exactly what opens nighttime exploration, supernatural encounters, and the mystery-driven main story. Neither choice is "wrong," but they produce very different games.

| Feature | Standard Experience | Village in the Sunshine |
|---|---|---|
| Village rules | Can be broken | Cannot be broken |
| Nighttime exploration | Available after curfew | Not part of the mode |
| Horror content | Present | Reduced — exact differences still unconfirmed |
| Story progression | Full mystery storyline | Peaceful daily life focus |
| Best for | Players who want the complete game | Players who want pure farming |

A practical compromise many players consider: start in Village in the Sunshine to learn the farming systems without pressure, then run the standard mode once you know the map. Just know that your choice is made at the beginning, so decide deliberately rather than by accident.

## Your First Week: A Realistic Priority Plan

The opening days are about building a loop you can repeat, not about maximizing anything. Here is a sensible order of operations for your first week in Kagatsu.

| Priority | What to Do | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Walk the village during daylight and note shops, fields, and exits | You cannot plan routes you cannot visualize |
| 2 | Clear and plant your starting plot | Your first harvest is your first real income |
| 3 | Secure basic tools and begin upgrading them | Better tools save in-game hours every single day |
| 4 | Establish a daily routine: water, forage, fish, sell | Consistency beats bursts in a season-based game |
| 5 | Start cooking as soon as you have spare ingredients | Cooked dishes are usually worth more than raw crops |
| 6 | Meet villagers and learn the village rules | Rules are the game's core system, not flavor text |
| 7 | Only then consider nighttime exploration | Nights are the riskiest content and the least forgiving |

**Common beginner mistakes to avoid:**

- Sprinting into nighttime content on day two, before you know the map or have supplies.
- Selling every raw ingredient instead of holding some back for cooking and crafting.
- Expanding your fields faster than your tools and stamina can support.
- Ignoring villagers early, then scrambling to build relationships later.
- Treating the village rules as optional lore when they are actually the progression system.

## Early Money, Tools, and Everyday Systems

Money in the early game comes from breadth, not depth. You will not get rich off a single crop in week one, but stacking several small income streams adds up quickly.

| Income Source | Effort | Beginner Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Selling crops | Low | Reliable and predictable once fields are running |
| Fishing | Low to medium | Depends on season, time, and location |
| Foraging | Low | Free resources while you walk between tasks |
| Cooking | Medium | Turns cheap ingredients into higher-value dishes |
| Hunting | Medium to high | Requires a hunting license before you can start |
| Livestock products | Medium | Daily care and affection drive output over time |

Tool and farm upgrades are where early money should go. A useful rule of thumb: upgrade whatever you use every single day first, then whatever unlocks new content. Watering and tilling tools pay for themselves in saved time long before decorative purchases do.

| Upgrade Priority | Target | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Daily-use farming tools | Used every morning without exception |
| 2 | Access and licenses | Opens hunting and new gathering routes |
| 3 | Processing and cooking | Raises the value of what you already grow |
| 4 | Home and furniture | Comfort and decoration, not early efficiency |

Your starting home and garden are deliberately neglected. That is a feature, not a bug — the slow transformation of a scrappy plot into a working farm is the emotional core of the daytime loop.

## Farming, Livestock, Cooking, and Hunting Basics

Each system feeds the next, so treat them as one connected loop rather than five separate chores.

| Activity | When It Fits | Beginner Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Farming | Morning, daily | Manage seasonal crops across fields, ridges, and rice paddies |
| Livestock | Morning, daily | Consistent care and affection matter more than bursts of attention |
| Fishing | Any free block | Match season, time, and location to what you are chasing |
| Hunting | Afternoon trips | Get the license early so you can gather meat and materials |
| Cooking | Evening | Convert surplus ingredients before they pile up |
| Crafting | Rainy days | Turn gathered resources into furniture and upgrades |

Crop quality is a long game. Fertilizer, scarecrows, and careful field management improve output over seasons, so do not judge your farm by its first harvest. Livestock work similarly — animals are evaluated, and steady care is what earns results. Community guides also reference village evaluations as a way to measure how well your farm is developing.

Village events and seasonal festivals round out the calendar. These are worth attending for the routine and the relationships even before you understand what they are foreshadowing.

## Village Rules, the 11 PM Curfew, and Nighttime Exploration

Official descriptions are blunt about three warnings: do not talk to strangers, do not leave the village, and do not go outside at night. Guide coverage further describes eight village rules and an 11 PM curfew, and it is the breaking of those rules that unlocks Kagatsu's darker systems.

| Official Warning | What It Suggests | Beginner Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Do not talk to strangers | Outsiders are connected to the mystery | Be cautious with unfamiliar faces |
| Do not leave the village | The village boundary matters | Learn where the exits are before testing them |
| Do not go outside at night | Night is where the horror lives | Prepare before you ever break curfew |

If you intend to explore after dark, treat it like a dungeon run rather than a stroll. Empty your inventory of anything you would hate to lose, carry whatever light source you have, learn a route home in daylight first, and save before you step out. Players who wander into the night unprepared tend to learn the hard way — and player experience consistently suggests the nighttime systems are far less forgiving than the daytime ones.

The eight rules and the curfew are the clearest example of how this game works: the rules exist so that breaking them means something.

## FAQ

**Is this Village in the Shade beginner guide useful for console players?**
Yes. Farming, tool, and rule systems are the same across PC, PS5, Switch, and Switch 2. Only minor interface differences separate the versions.

**Should I start with Village in the Sunshine or the standard mode?**
Start with the standard mode if you want the complete story and the horror atmosphere. Choose Village in the Sunshine if you want a relaxed farming game and would rather not deal with rule-breaking content at all.

**How soon can I explore at night?**
You can break curfew early, but you should not. Spend your first week learning the map, building income, and improving tools so that a night trip is a choice rather than a gamble.

**Is Village in the Shade actually scary?**
It carries official horror warnings and mature themes, and the nighttime content is where that tone lives. Village in the Sunshine is the safer option, though how much it reduces is still being confirmed by players.

The short version of this Village in the Shade beginner guide: build your daytime loop first, spend your early money on tools you use daily, and treat the village rules as the spine of the game rather than a warning label. Kagatsu rewards patience — and it notices when you do not have any.
