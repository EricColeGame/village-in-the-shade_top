## Why the Village in the Shade Achievements Are Worth Chasing

Farming games are generous with busywork but stingy with direction. Once the first season wraps and your fields are humming, it's easy to lose the thread of what to do next. That's exactly where the Village in the Shade achievements earn their keep: they function as a built-in checklist that nudges you toward systems you'd otherwise ignore — the processor, the field guide, the badminton court, the summer festival stalls.

Chasing Village in the Shade achievements also tells you something about how other players approach the game. Because Steam publishes global completion percentages, the list doubles as a heat map of where the community stalls out. The early shipping and mining goals are nearly universal; the tool upgrades and workbench grind are genuinely rare.

This guide breaks down the tracked Village in the Shade achievements, ranks them by difficulty, and gives you a practical order to work through them without wasting in-game days.

## The Fastest Unlocks: Start With These Six

If you want momentum, front-load the achievements that fire almost automatically. Five Pecks of Rice, the 10,000-yen shipping goal, is the single most common unlock — nearly four in five players have it, which means it pops during ordinary play. Pair it with Do Process by routing every harvestable through the processor before selling, and you'll clear two entries in the same routine.

Living off the Land and Field Guide Greenhorn overlap heavily too. Every forageable you pick up counts toward the 500-gather target *and* often adds a field guide entry. Yours, Mine, and Ores rewards the boulder-clearing you're already doing to open up farmland.

| Achievement | Rough Target | Best Habit to Build |
|---|---|---|
| Five Pecks of Rice | 10,000 yen shipped | Sell something every single day |
| Do Process | 100 processor uses | Process before you sell, always |
| Living off the Land | 500 forageables | Sweep the map edges each morning |
| Field Guide Greenhorn | 300 entries | Pick up one of everything you see |
| Yours, Mine, and Ores | 1,000 boulders | Clear rock clusters while expanding fields |
| Doing Good Business | 100,000 yen shipped | Chain the first goal into this one |

## The Long Grind: Counter Achievements Explained

Most of the Village in the Shade achievements are pure counters, and counters reward consistency over bursts. A player who cooks one meal per in-game day will hit Kitchen Duty long before someone who saves up ingredients for a single marathon session — because the goal is 100 *cooking actions*, not 100 dishes sold.

The same logic applies to crafting. Dr. DIY asks for 500 workbench crafts and sits at the bottom of the list at 24.4%, which suggests most players simply stop crafting once their farm is functional. If you're going for a full clear, keep a cheap, renewable recipe on hand and burn spare materials on it whenever you pass the bench.

| Counter Achievement | Target | Practical Farming Method |
|---|---|---|
| Plowman | 1,000 tilled squares | Till fresh ground daily; don't re-till the same plot |
| Chop Till You Drop | 1,000 trees or logs | Clear a forest patch per session, replant behind you |
| Petting Zoo | 500 animal interactions | Pet every animal morning and evening |
| Angler Devil | 300 fish | Fish the same spot while waiting on crops |
| For the Birds | 100 bird drops | Leave dropped items on the ground until you're nearby |
| Kitchen Duty | 100 cooks | Cook a trivial recipe daily |
| Dr. DIY | 500 workbench crafts | Craft in bulk from surplus materials |
| Put a Bow on It | 100 bow hunts | Take a bow on every foraging trip |

## The Rarest Village in the Shade Achievements

Four entries sit below 30% completion, and each one has a distinct reason for being uncommon. Understanding *why* they're rare is the fastest way to avoid the same trap.

| Achievement | Completion | Why Players Miss It |
|---|---|---|
| Dr. DIY | 24.4% | 500 crafts is a huge number for a system most players abandon early |
| Gifts From Above | 26.2% | Requires meteoric iron for *every* tool, so it's gated behind rare material |
| Summertime Spender | 27.7% | Tied to a seasonal event with a limited purchase window |
| Chop Till You Drop | 27.7% | 1,000 trees is slow unless you're actively clearing land |

Gifts From Above is the standout. Because it demands meteoric iron across your entire tool set, it can't be rushed — you need to find the material repeatedly and commit it to upgrades instead of selling it. Community reports suggest banking meteoric iron the moment you find it, rather than deciding later whether to spend it.

Summertime Spender is the one that punishes inattention. Ten purchases at the Summer Festival means you need to actually attend the festival and spend there, not just walk through it. Budget for it in advance so the window doesn't close on you.

### The Skill-Based Oddballs

Three entries aren't counters at all: Tater Toaster (roast a sweet potato), Mochi to be Desired (make mochi), and More Like Goodminton (return 15 shots in a single badminton rally). The first two are simple once you know the recipes exist — the trick is discovering them in the first place, so check your cooking options regularly and talk to villagers who mention seasonal foods.

More Like Goodminton is the only genuine execution test in the group. Fifteen consecutive returns requires rhythm more than speed; stay centered and let the rally develop rather than lunging for winners.

## A Practical Order for a Full Clear

Trying to do everything at once is how players burn out. A layered approach keeps progress visible.

| Phase | Focus | Achievements Covered |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Normal play, sell daily, process everything | Five Pecks of Rice, Do Process, Doing Good Business |
| 2 | Forage and log everything you find | Living off the Land, Field Guide Greenhorn |
| 3 | Expand farmland, clear rocks and trees | Yours, Mine, and Ores, Plowman, Chop Till You Drop |
| 4 | Daily chores: animals, cooking, crafting, fishing | Petting Zoo, Kitchen Duty, Dr. DIY, Angler Devil |
| 5 | Seasonal and rare-material pushes | Summertime Spender, Gifts From Above, Field Guide Genius |

A few habits make the whole list go faster:

- **Keep a running task board.** Note which counters are closest to finishing and finish them first — closing out a counter removes it from your mental load.
- **Don't sell meteoric iron.** It's the bottleneck for the rarest achievement on the list.
- **Cook and craft in small daily batches.** Ten cooks across ten days beats one long session, because you'll keep ingredients flowing.
- **Revisit the field guide often.** Going from 300 to 500 entries is the biggest single jump on the list; treat new items as collectibles.
- **Check the [full Village in the Shade achievement list on Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/3934250/achievements)** for the entries without public stats, since those are the ones guides tend to skip.

## FAQ

**How many Village in the Shade achievements are there?**
The Steam list totals 35. Twenty of them currently display public global completion percentages, and those are the ones covered in detail above — the rest are visible on the store page but don't show community stats.

**Do I have to play a specific mode to unlock them?**
Yes. The descriptions specify Village in the Shade Mode for these goals, so keep your achievement hunting inside that mode rather than splitting time across others.

**Which Village in the Shade achievement should I save for last?**
Gifts From Above is the natural finale. Upgrading every tool with meteoric iron requires repeated finds of a rare material, so it's best treated as a long-term background goal rather than a dedicated session.

**Can I work on several achievements at once?**
Absolutely, and you should. Foraging feeds the field guide, processing feeds shipping income, and tilling feeds your farm's expansion — most of the list overlaps naturally if you build the right daily routine.
