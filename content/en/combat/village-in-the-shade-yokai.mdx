## How the Village in the Shade Yokai Threat Loop Works

The marketing line does a lot of heavy lifting: *abide by the rules*. That phrasing implies a conditional world — one where the countryside is genuinely calm until a specific condition is violated. In practice, that usually means the danger is authored, not random. Yokai are not wandering aimlessly waiting for a hitbox collision; they are answering something you did.

That reframes the entire "combat" question. If the threat is triggered by behavior, then the most powerful tool in your kit is not a weapon — it is information. Knowing which action flips the world from pastoral to predatory is worth more than any damage output.

| Phase | What You Can Usually Do | What to Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Morning / midday | Farm, forage, talk to villagers, explore freely | NPC dialogue that hints at local customs |
| Late afternoon | Wrap up chores, stock supplies, plan a route home | Time pressure — the map gets less forgiving |
| Night | Move carefully, stay indoors, avoid open ground | Sightlines, sound cues, light sources |
| After breaking a rule | Everything above, but with pursuit | Escape routes and hiding spots |

### The Rule Itself Is the Enemy

Treat every rule as a boss mechanic with a tell. Villagers will mention customs casually — "we don't answer the door after dark," "nobody walks the shrine path on the thirteenth." Those are not flavor text. They are patch notes for the encounter system.

Write them down. A physical list beats memory when the game is actively trying to unnerve you.

## Fight, Hide, or Flee: Choosing Your Response

Here is where most newcomers get hurt. They treat every yokai as a combat encounter and every combat encounter as winnable. In rule-based horror design, that assumption is usually wrong — and expensive.

| Response | Best When | Main Risk | Relative Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confront | The yokai has a clear, exploitable weakness you have already observed | Wasted resources and a longer recovery | High |
| Hide | Pursuit is active and you have a valid hiding spot nearby | Being found mid-hide with no exit | Low |
| Flee | You are in open ground with a known route back to safety | Stamina drain and getting cornered | Low to medium |
| Comply | A rule is being enforced and you can still satisfy it | Timing pressure | Lowest |

The pattern worth internalizing: **comply first, flee second, hide third, confront last.** Player experience with rule-driven horror consistently shows that the "fight" option exists, but it is a scalpel, not a hammer. Use it when you have already learned the pattern — not as a panic response.

### Testing Without Dying

You need data, but you do not want to feed the game your entire evening. Run controlled tests:

- Approach a known yokai zone with a full inventory and a mapped escape route.
- Change exactly one variable per attempt — light, distance, time of night.
- Note whether the response was aggro, chase, or ignore.
- Retreat the moment the behavior shifts from idle to alert.

Three clean tests teach you more than ten panicked deaths.

### The Three-Second Rule

Before you cross any threshold — a gate, a shrine step, a doorway — stop and ask three questions. Is it dark? Is a rule active? Do I have an exit? If any answer is bad, do not cross. This single habit prevents more deaths than any gear upgrade.

## Reading Yokai Behavior: Patterns Worth Learning

NIS has kept specifics close to the chest, so treat the cues below as a diagnostic framework rather than a datamined bestiary. Watch for the shift from ambient to aware.

| Behavior Cue | Likely Meaning | Correct Response |
|---|---|---|
| Yokai holds position, faces you | It has noticed you but is not committed | Back away slowly, break line of sight |
| Yokai moves parallel to you | Tracking, waiting for a rule to trigger | Do not run — running confirms pursuit |
| Audio pitch rises, ambience thins | Escalation state | Get indoors or to a lit area immediately |
| Yokai repeats a fixed path | Scripted patrol | Time your movement, do not fight it |
| Multiple entities converge | You have already broken a rule | Comply or flee; confrontation is a trap |

The most common mistake is treating stillness as safety. In this genre, a yokai that stops moving is usually the most dangerous one on screen.

They are the supernatural threat tied to the game's rule system — spirits that respond when the village's customs are violated. NIS has framed the whole experience around conditional peace, which means the yokai are less random monsters and more enforcers of local rules.
