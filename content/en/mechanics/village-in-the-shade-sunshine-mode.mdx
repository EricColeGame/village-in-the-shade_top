## What "Sunshine Mode" Actually Means in Village in the Shade

If you've seen the phrase bouncing around forums, Discord servers, and comment sections, here's the short version: there is no menu toggle literally labeled "Sunshine Mode." The feature players are describing is the game's official **Peaceful Life Mode**, which you pick when you begin a new file. Community reports and streamer chatter started calling it "sunshine mode" because of what it does — it turns a game about a mountain village with a terrifying code into a straightforward, cozy farming sim. So when people search for a Village in the Shade sunshine mode, they're really asking one question: can I play this without the scares? The answer is yes, and it's built in.

The distinction matters because the term isn't in the game's menus, and that causes confusion. Someone hears "sunshine mode," boots up the title, scans the options screen, and finds nothing by that name — then assumes the feature doesn't exist. Understanding that Village in the Shade sunshine mode is player shorthand for Peaceful Life Mode saves you that scavenger hunt.

## What Actually Changes When You Play Sunshine Mode

Kagatsu Village is a mountain community governed by a code — a set of rules passed down through generations. In standard play, breaking that code can trigger something genuinely unsettling. That tension is the hook for a lot of players, and it's the reason the game comes from the makers of the Yomawari series.

Peaceful Life Mode flips that off at the source. Rather than letting you break a rule and then softening the punishment, it removes your ability to break the code at all. You're not dodging consequences; the consequences never enter the picture.

| Element | Standard Play | Village in the Shade Sunshine Mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The village code | Active — violations are possible | You cannot break it |
| Horror payoff | Terrifying events can occur | The rule-breaking path is closed |
| Farming and livestock | Yes | Yes |
| Fishing and hunting | Yes | Yes |
| Villager relationships | Yes | Yes |
| House and garden renovation | Yes | Yes |
| Seasonal changes | Yes | Yes |
| Overall tone | Cozy with dread underneath | Cozy throughout |

One honest caveat: treat "completely horror-free" as community shorthand rather than a studio guarantee about every single scene. The official wording focuses specifically on preventing code violations and their fallout. If absolute zero spookiness matters to you, that's worth knowing going in.

## What You Can Still Do in Sunshine Mode

This is where newcomers relax: the mode doesn't cut content. It cuts threat. Everything that makes Village in the Shade a life simulation still works.

- **Farming** — till fields, plant, and harvest across the seasons
- **Livestock** — raise animals as part of your daily routine
- **Fishing** — cast a line and bring home dinner or profit
- **Hunting** — an activity available in the standard loop, not a horror mechanic
- **Villager bonds** — talk to residents, build relationships, become part of the community
- **Home renovation** — take a run-down house and an overgrown garden and turn them into something you're proud of
- **Seasonal exploration** — watch the mountain village change around you

The loop is the same loop. What disappears is the anxiety of wondering whether your next decision trips a rule you didn't know existed.

## Practical Tips for a Great Sunshine Run

A few habits make Peaceful Life Mode noticeably better.

**Learn the code anyway.** Even though you can't break it in this mode, knowing the rules gives you context for why villagers behave the way they do. It also makes a second, standard-mode playthrough far less blindsiding.

**Renovate early, not last.** Your house and garden are a long-term project. Starting on them in your first season means you actually get to enjoy the result rather than rushing it at the end.

**Build relationships on a schedule.** Villagers are the heart of the game, and bonds deepen over repeated contact. A quick daily round of conversations costs little and compounds fast.

**Rotate your income sources.** Farming, livestock, fishing, and hunting all feed into the same economy. Spreading your effort across them smooths out the lean stretches of a season.

**Don't treat it as the "lesser" mode.** Community reports are pretty consistent on this point: plenty of players prefer sunshine mode precisely because the farming, decorating, and relationship systems are strong enough to carry the game on their own.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is there an official sunshine mode in Village in the Shade?**
Not by that name. "Sunshine mode" is community shorthand for Peaceful Life Mode, the official option you select at the start of the game. Functionally, a Village in the Shade sunshine mode playthrough is a Peaceful Life Mode playthrough — same thing, different label.

**Can I switch to Peaceful Life Mode in the middle of a save?**
The official wording frames the choice as something you make at the start of the game, so plan on deciding up front rather than toggling later. If you're on the fence, start peaceful and add a second file for standard play.

**Does sunshine mode remove farming, fishing, or villager relationships?**
No. Those systems are untouched. The mode specifically closes off the ability to break Kagatsu Village's code, which is what removes the terrifying outcomes. The life simulation content stays intact.

**Is Peaceful Life Mode too easy or boring?**
That depends entirely on what you want. If the horror hook is why you're here, standard play is the draw. If you came for seasonal farming, home renovation, and small-town relationships, community reports suggest the peaceful route holds up on its own.
